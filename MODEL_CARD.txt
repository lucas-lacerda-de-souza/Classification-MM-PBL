🧠 Model Card — Multimodal AI for Multiple Myeloma (MM) vs Plasmablastic Lymphoma (PBL)
1. Model Overview
This repository provides a comprehensive and explainable multimodal artificial intelligence framework developed to support the differential diagnosis between Multiple Myeloma (MM) and 
Plasmablastic Lymphoma (PBL) using routine H&E-stained whole-slide images (WSIs) in combination with clinicopathological variables and nuclear morphometric features.
MM and PBL represent a diagnostically challenging pair of plasma-cell neoplasms due to substantial overlap in morphology, plasmablastic differentiation, and high-grade cytological features, 
particularly in small or fragmented biopsy specimens. The proposed system aims to reduce diagnostic ambiguity and interobserver variability by integrating complementary sources of information
within a unified computational pipeline.

- The multimodal system integrates:
Deep learning patch-level classifiers
AlexNet
ResNet50
DenseNet121
InceptionV3
CellViT (Vision Transformer–based architecture)

- Nuclear morphometric analysis (area, perimeter, circularity, eccentricity, hematoxylin optical density)

- Traditional machine learning
XGBoost classifier for clinicopathological and morphometric feature selection

- Multimodal late-fusion models
Joint integration of CNN/Transformer embeddings with structured clinical variables

- Explainability tools
Grad-CAM for spatial interpretability
SHAP for feature attribution and transparency

The overarching goal is to develop an assistive diagnostic tool capable of supporting pathologists in the classification of MM and PBL, particularly in diagnostically equivocal cases.

2. Data Used
- Source and Composition
Total cases: 62
31 Multiple Myeloma (MM)
31 Plasmablastic Lymphoma (PBL)

- Anatomical focus: Head and neck region
Data origin: Nine international centres across Brazil, Mexico, Guatemala, and the United Kingdom
Time span: 2000–2024

- Ethics approvals:
University of Campinas (Brazil): Protocol no. 67064422.9.1001.5418
West of Scotland Research Ethics Service: 20/WS/0017
All diagnoses were confirmed according to the most recent WHO classification of haematolymphoid neoplasms and independently reviewed by three expert pathologists.

- Image Acquisition and Preparation
Scanning systems:
Aperio CS Digital Pathology System (Leica Biosystems)
Pannoramic 250 (3DHISTECH)
Magnification: 40×
Patch size: 299 × 299 pixels
Patch overlap: 20%

- Pre-processing Pipeline
Manual ROI delineation to retain diagnostically relevant tissue
Semantic segmentation to remove background and artefacts
Macenko colour normalisation to mitigate inter-institutional staining variability
Patient-level data splitting to prevent data leakage:
Training: 80%
Validation: 10%
Test: 10%

- Data Augmentation (Training Only)
Rotations (90°, 180°, 270°)
Gaussian blur
Zoom (+20%)

- Clinical & Morphometric Variables
Extracted and evaluated variables included:
Age
Sex
Anatomical site
Nuclear area
Nuclear perimeter
Nuclear circularity
Nuclear eccentricity
Mean hematoxylin optical density

Only variables demonstrating meaningful contribution in SHAP analysis were retained for multimodal integration.

- External Validation
Two independent external validation cohorts were used, consisting exclusively of cases from centres not included in the training phase.

3. Model Performance Summary
🧬 Morphometric Analysis
Statistically significant differences between MM and PBL nuclei were observed:
Lower nuclear circularity in MM: p = 0.0220
Higher nuclear eccentricity in MM: p = 0.0255
Trend towards larger nuclear area in MM: p = 0.0616
No significant differences were found for:
Nuclear perimeter: p = 0.8366
Mean hematoxylin optical density: p = 0.2718
These findings indicate increased nuclear elongation and morphological irregularity in MM compared with PBL.

5. Intended Use
This model is intended for:
Diagnostic support and triage in challenging MM vs PBL cases
Reduction of interobserver variability
Research and educational applications in computational pathology
Morphological exploration via explainable AI tools
Not intended for autonomous clinical diagnosis.

6. Limitations
Moderate cohort size due to disease rarity
Lack of molecular and immunophenotypic integration
Patch-based inference may miss global architectural context
Grad-CAM provides indirect approximations of human reasoning

7. Potential Biases
Geographic and institutional staining variability
ROI-based sampling bias
Class balance maintained at patient level only

8. Ethical & Responsible AI Considerations
Data anonymisation and ethical approvals obtained
Explainability incorporated to enhance transparency
Outputs are assistive and must be interpreted by trained professionals
Further prospective multicentre validation is required
